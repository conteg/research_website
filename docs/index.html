Family and labor economics

Giorgia Conte

Postdoctoral Researcher
Norwegian School of Economics (NHH)

I study how public policy shapes work, family decisions, and demographic change.

My research combines dynamic structural macroeconomic models with empirical analysis of microdata to explore questions in family and labor economics.

I hold a PhD in Economics from Trinity College Dublin, supervised by Joseph Kopecky and Barra Roantree.

The Distributional Effects of Paid Parental Leave Policies on Fertility and Children's Human Capital

How do fertility responses to paid parental leave shape parental investment and children’s human capital?

Read paper
Abstract

This paper combines microdata evidence from New Jersey with a heterogeneous-agent model of endogenous fertility and parental investment. Paid leave increases higher-order births, particularly among middle-income families and less-educated women. Fertility responses dilute investment per child and are central to the policy’s effects on children’s human capital.

02

Parental Leave Policies and Fertility: The Emotional Cost of Maternal Care

How does family support mediate the effects of longer parental leave on subsequent births?

Best Paper Award · ISEWE and IEA · IEA Annual Conference 2024

Read paper
Abstract

Using a Danish parental leave reform, this paper examines how informal care networks shape fertility responses. The effects of longer leave differ with the availability of grandmother support, highlighting the role of non-monetary costs of maternal care in family policy.

03

Welcome to the Gray Parade: The Welfare Economics of Shrinking Populations

With Joseph Kopecky (Trinity College Dublin)

Comparing later retirement and fertility policies in an economy facing demographic decline.

Read paper
Abstract

An overlapping-generations model calibrated to South Korea compares policies that extend working lives with subsidies that reduce the cost of children. The analysis finds that fertility policy can complement adaptation through later retirement, with implications for welfare and pension financing.
